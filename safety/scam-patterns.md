# Common scam patterns

Students looking for internships, fellowships and jobs are a common target for fraud. This page lists the **patterns**
to watch for. It deliberately names no organisation or website: a scheme can change its name overnight, but the
pattern stays the same.

**The one rule:** a real programme or employer never asks you to pay for a seat, an offer letter, a certificate or
"processing". If money comes before the offer, stop.

## Warning signs

| Pattern | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Pay to be selected** | A "registration", "security deposit", "training" or "kit" fee before or after a "selection". Real internships and jobs pay you; they don't charge you. |
| **Guaranteed placement or admission** | "100% placement", "guaranteed visa", "assured admission abroad" in return for a package fee. No one can guarantee another organisation's decision. |
| **Fake offer letter** | An offer without an interview, often as a PDF with a well-known logo, followed by a request for a deposit or for documents. |
| **Chat-only recruiters** | First contact on WhatsApp or Telegram, a personal number, "work from home, earn per task", or a group that moves you to another app. |
| **Lookalike websites and e-mail** | A domain one letter off a real one, a free e-mail address claiming to be a company or university, or a form that copies an official page. |
| **"Fellowships" with no host** | A fellowship or "research internship" with no named university, lab or supervisor, where the main outcome is a paid certificate. |
| **Certificate and publication mills** | Paid certificates, "international awards", or guaranteed publication in a journal or conference you have never heard of. |
| **Early requests for sensitive data** | Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, card numbers or OTPs before a written, verifiable offer. Never share an OTP with anyone. |
| **Pressure** | "Only 2 seats left", "pay within 24 hours", or anger when you ask questions. |

## How to check an opportunity

1. **Find it yourself.** Go to the organisation's official website from a search engine (not from the link you were
   sent) and look for the same programme or role on its own careers or programme page.
2. **Check the sender.** The e-mail should come from the organisation's own domain. Recruiters for real roles can say
   which team they are hiring for and use the company's own job board.
3. **Look for the money.** Any fee, deposit or "refundable" amount before you start is a red flag.
4. **Search for complaints.** Search the name with words like "fraud" or "scam", and ask students or alumni who were
   part of it.
5. **Ask.** Open a [caution report](../../../issues/new/choose) here, or check the warnings in the app:
   [roziqair.vercel.app/warnings](https://roziqair.vercel.app/warnings).

## If you've been targeted

- Stop paying and stop sharing documents. Keep the messages, receipts and payment details.
- In India, report it at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in),
  or call the cyber fraud helpline **1930** as soon as you can: early reports give the best chance of stopping a
  payment.
- Tell your bank straight away if you paid or shared card or bank details.
- Warn others: a caution report here helps the next student.

This page is general guidance, not legal advice.
